## T-001 — Toby Crabel

Evidence grade: `A-`

### Success/longevity evidence
- Crabel Capital Management says it was founded in 1987 and has delivered more than three decades of uncorrelated
  institutional returns.
- Firm-reported AUM was about $5B as of 2026-02-01.
- Public CTA databases list active systematic programs with long histories.
- Current firm success cannot be attributed solely to Crabel's 1990 book rules; the firm has evolved substantially.

### What he observes
- traded price -> bar -> wave -> cumulative volume/volatility -> market cycle;
- volatility contraction / expansion;
- trend vs trading range;
- breakout success vs failure;
- liquidity runs;
- wave thrust, persistence, shortening;
- effort relative to result.

### Key 2026 research philosophy
Crabel explicitly writes:

```text
Observation
-> Pattern
-> Concept
-> Principle
-> Statistics
-> Strategy
```

He argues statistics should evaluate concepts rather than replace conceptual understanding.

Examples he gives:
- NR7 -> possible volatility contraction;
- opening-range expansion -> possible market imbalance;
- gap continuation -> possible inventory adjustment.

### Mechanism hypothesis
Patterns are compressed observations of recurring market states.

A breakout is not meaningful because it has a name. It is meaningful if:
- prior balance/contraction exists;
- price leaves it;
- interaction/follow-through demonstrates imbalance;
- failure/re-entry instead reveals rejection/trapped positioning.

### What to import
- conceptual hierarchy;
- contraction/expansion as state;
- breakout success/failure as a central transition;
- wave character;
- adaptation as market structure evolves.

### What NOT to import
- any fixed NR4/NR7/ORB threshold as timeless truth;
- Wyckoff labels as privileged ground truth;
- statements like "80% of breakouts fail" without our own population definition and validation.

## T-002 — Linda Bradford Raschke

Evidence grade: `A-/B+`

### Success/longevity evidence
- roughly 45-year trading career reported in a 2025 MarketWatch profile;
- former exchange market maker and CTA;
- founded Granat hedge fund;
- MarketWatch reports Granat ranked #17 of 4,500 hedge funds for five-year return by Barclay Hedge.

### What she observes
- opening price relative to previous range;
- trend context;
- how price behaves at anticipated support/levels;
- rare conditional price patterns;
- whether a move is normal mean-reversion or an outlier/momentum event.

### Research method
She describes her setups as `principles of price behavior` and advocates basic conditional modeling:
- given a specific path through the day, what happens next?
- how often does price close at an extreme?
- how often does it recover?

She explicitly distinguishes this from blindly converting every observation into one mechanical system.

### Mechanism hypothesis
The setup is conditional on regime.

The same apparent overextension:
- may mean mean reversion in an ordinary state;
- must not be faded when a genuine outlier/momentum process is unfolding.

### What to import
- context-dependent strategy selection;
- event/path modeling rather than isolated indicator values;
- daily scenario planning;
- explicit no-trade / do-not-fade regimes.

### What NOT to import
- discretionary intuition as an untestable black box;
- any exact proprietary setup not sufficiently documented.

## T-003 — Peter Brandt

Evidence grade: `B+`

### Success/longevity evidence
- 40+ years as a futures/FX trader;
- publishes detailed proprietary process statistics;
- self-reported long-run win rate since 1981 of ~42%;
- reports >80% of profits from <20% of trades.

Performance specifics are primarily self-reported, so they are not treated like audited fund history.

### What he observes
- classical horizontal chart structures;
- rectangles;
- properly diagnosed H&S;
- right-angle triangles;
- flags/pennants only in strong ongoing trends;
- congestion vs active trend;
- breakout completion;
- reward/risk asymmetry.

### What he says patterns represent
In a CFA Magazine interview:
- H&S top is described as a possible distribution process;
- rectangle represents supply/demand equilibrium;
- breakout from equilibrium signals a change in balance of power.

He explicitly says charts do not predict price for him; they organize:
- trend;
- congestion vs active trend;
- asymmetric trade possibility.

### Payoff architecture
- max risk ~1% nominal trading capital per trade;
- stop based on entry + protective level;
- aggressive stop advancement;
- allow rare winners to dominate;
- H&S proprietary historical payoff ratio reported around 2.15:1.

### Mechanism hypothesis
A mature pattern is a compressed record of repeated failure/progress around a boundary.

Completion matters because:
- the market must actually resolve the prior balance;
- invalidation is visible;
- payoff geometry becomes asymmetric.

### What to import
- proper pattern diagnosis before naming;
- balance/distribution interpretation;
- completion/confirmation;
- possibility tree rather than certainty;
- explicit invalidation and payoff.

### What NOT to import
- H&S shape detector as an Entry signal;
- 42% WR as acceptable final V5 production objective.

## T-004 — Richard Dennis / William Eckhardt / Turtle lineage

Evidence grade: `A-/B+`

### Success/longevity evidence
- real-capital training experiment beginning 1983;
- public historical accounts report large aggregate Turtle profits;
- individual Turtle performance records are available in secondary historical compilations;
- trend-following as a broader strategy family has independent academic evidence across markets and long history.

### What they observe
- price breakout;
- volatility;
- current equity;
- system orientation;
- risk aversion.

### System architecture
The Turtle rules are not "20-day high = magic signal."

They combine:
- market universe;
- breakout entries;
- volatility-based position sizing;
- stops;
- exits;
- portfolio risk;
- pyramiding/diversification.

Eckhardt is repeatedly quoted arguing that liquidation criteria can matter more than initiation.

### Mechanism hypothesis
The strategy does not need to identify a reversal bottom/top.

It waits for evidence that price is already escaping its prior state, then:
- cuts failed attempts;
- remains exposed to persistent trends;
- lets rare directional expansions dominate the distribution.

This maps naturally to:
`boundary acceptance -> persistent expansion`.

### Academic cross-check
Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (JFE 2012) document time-series momentum across 58 liquid futures/forwards with return
persistence at roughly 1-12 month horizons.

Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen extend trend-following evidence back to 1880.

### What to import
- confirmation over prediction;
- volatility-normalized risk;
- lifecycle/exit authority;
- diversified capture of fat tails.

### What NOT to import
- the exact 20/55-day rules as a modern intraday GOLD strategy;
- historical gross performance as evidence that our execution environment shares the same edge.

# Corpus V1 synthesis

Across discretionary and systematic traders, the recurring hierarchy is:

```text
STATE
-> LOCATION
-> APPROACH
-> INTERACTION
-> RESPONSE
-> INVALIDATION
-> PAYOFF / LIFECYCLE
```

The most important V5 implication is that a named pattern is usually **not the bottom-level cause**.

Examples:

```text
rectangle
-> balance/equilibrium
-> boundary resolution

H&S
-> attempted continuation / distribution narrative
-> failed progress + boundary completion

NR7
-> volatility contraction
-> later state transition

breakout
-> attempt at price discovery
-> acceptance OR rejection

liquidity run
-> boundary penetration / stop activation
-> continuation OR failure depending on subsequent acceptance
```

Therefore V5-001 begins with the boundary interaction itself.
